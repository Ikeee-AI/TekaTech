## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** category-interchangeable. Sage green on white rounded cards, a left filter rail and a card grid could front any SaaS catalog. TekaTech's real differentiator, answers you can check against official sources, is invisible until you open a gadget and ask a question: the catalog never shows evidence, source counts or "AI-ready" status to the shopper. The one strong idea, numbered citations that jump to the source, is the product's character and it isn't expressed anywhere in the visual language.

**Deterministic scan:** not run (owner kept the detector binary off).

## Overall Impression

Functionally solid and honest about its states, but it reads as an admin tool that shoppers are allowed into. **The biggest opportunity:** make "sourced and checkable" the visual identity, and cut the catalog down to what a shopper needs to choose.

## What's Working

1. **Honest AI states.** Grounded answer, no evidence, general knowledge (labelled) and failures are distinct, with retry. This is rare and worth keeping as the visual centrepiece.
2. **Suggested questions** on the gadget page ("What chip does it use?", "How long does the battery last?") match the shopper's real questions and lower the blank-box problem.
3. **Clear AI-readiness feedback** for admins ("Ready for AI", "All 5 specs are ready") with a source domain link on each spec card.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Catalog cards are oversized and action-heavy.** Each card has 5 controls (Add to My Gadgets, Compare, Delete, Ask AI, Details) plus a 3-cell info strip that repeats the brand. On a 390 px phone each card is 450 px tall, and the **first card starts at 841 px**, below the filters, the catalog heading and the popular-question list.
*Why it matters:* a shopper scanning a dozen phones sees one per screen and has to parse five buttons on each.
*Fix:* make the whole card the link to Details; keep Compare and a small "save" icon as the only extra actions; move Delete to the gadget page; drop the duplicate brand; collapse popular questions behind one chip row.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable distill` then `/impeccable layout`.

**[P1] One condensed typeface does every job.** Everything uses Open Sans at 75% width (`font-stretch: 75%`, `--font-display` = `--font-mono` = `--font-sans`). Headings, body, prices and spec numbers all look the same, and helper text is 11–12 px grey.
*Why it matters:* spec numbers (mAh, Hz, ₱) are what shoppers compare. Condensed small type makes them harder to read and to tell apart, especially on phones.
*Fix:* a readable normal-width body face, a distinct display face, and tabular figures for specs and prices; raise the minimum helper text size.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable typeset`.

**[P1] No visual identity tied to the product.** The look could belong to any product; nothing says "official sources, checkable facts". Citations, source domains and "verified" dates are TekaTech's proof, but they appear only deep in the gadget page.
*Why it matters:* the rebrand's job is to make the trust mechanism what people remember.
*Fix:* choose a visual world around evidence (source marks, citation numerals as a motif, spec "proof" treatment) and show source/AI-ready signals on catalog cards.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable shape` (rebrand direction), then new-work.

**[P2] Admin and operations language leaks to shoppers.** Shoppers see "Use Index for AI when credit is available; pending specs remain readable here", "5 parts", "Verified 3 weeks ago" (it is the saved date) and "Php 84,990".
*Why it matters:* it confuses non-experts and makes the product feel unfinished.
*Fix:* show indexing text to admins only; "From samsung.com · checked 23 Oct"; ₱ with a thin space; hide "parts".
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable clarify`.

**[P2] The gadget page hides the evidence.** Above the fold: a large grey "No Photo" box and "Not listed" twice. The specs, the evidence the AI cites, start about 1.5 screens down, and their format is inconsistent: only "Rear cameras" has a label column.
*Why it matters:* the page's strongest content sits under the weakest.
*Fix:* a compact header when there is no photo; key specs as a scannable labelled table near the top; consistent label/value rows.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable layout`.

## Persona Red Flags

**Liza, a first-time buyer in Manila on a mid-range Android phone, choosing between two phones under ₱25,000** (project-specific, from `PRODUCT.md`): she scrolls past filters and popular questions before seeing any phone. Then she gets one 450 px card per screen with five buttons, and has to tick Compare on two cards far apart. She sees "Php" and "Not listed" prices and can't sort by what matters to her (battery, camera).

**Jordan (first-timer):** "Index for AI", "parts" and "Allow General Knowledge" have no explanation in the place they appear. Jordan doesn't know what General Knowledge changes or why it's off.

**Sam (accessibility):** helper text at 11–12 px grey on white, condensed type throughout, and the filter rail is a nested scroll area inside the scrolling page (two scrollbars side by side). The day-to-day keyboard paths work.

## Minor Observations

- Filter rail: the category and brand lists scroll inside a box that itself scrolls; test data ("Ewdewe", "12", "121", "Test") shows in production-like filters on dev only.
- The header's "Signed in as <email>" pill uses a lot of space next to the avatar, which already opens the account page.
- The compare empty state is small and floats high on an empty page; it could show how to start (tick 2–3).
- The popular-question chips show raw counts ("×11") without saying what they count.

## Questions to Consider

- What if the catalog led with "compare two phones" instead of a filter rail?
- What would a card look like if its main job was to show *how well-sourced* a gadget is?
- Does a shopper ever need Delete or Ask AI on a card, or only once they're on the gadget page?
- What would a confident, evidence-first TekaTech look like next to a retailer's spec page?
